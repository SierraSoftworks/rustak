# M10-18 — A rate limiter with fixed memory and no contended lock: two tiers of count-min sketch

**Why — maintainer's design decision, 2026-10-01.** The limiter is one `Mutex<HashMap<(address, subject), Bucket>>` (`rustak-server/src/auth/ratelimit.rs`). Three weaknesses, all DoS vectors against the sign-in path:
- **A scan under the lock.** Once the map is at its 100,000-entry ceiling, `make_room` runs a full `retain` on every new-key insert (found by M10-17), so each attacker failure during a flood costs a 100,000-entry scan that blocks every sign-in. Every `check` takes the same lock even for a key that has never failed.
- **Memory bounded by entries, not bytes**, with an attacker-chosen `String` in the key; and one address can mint a bucket per username.
- **Eviction forgives.** A flood that fills the map evicts, so lockouts can be washed out.

The maintainer chose a count-min sketch in two tiers, after Stochastic Fair Blue (a caller is refused only when *every* one of its cells says so) — fixed memory by construction, no keys stored, nothing to evict, no scans, lock-free:
- **Tier 1 — the address.** IPv4 at /32; IPv6 truncated to /64, and counted again at /48 with a higher threshold (a hosting customer's /48 is 65,536 distinct /64s). Rejects a caller who is flooding the system, whatever they are guessing at.
- **Tier 2 — the (address, subject) pair**, the address truncated the same way. This is the account-level limit, and keeping the address in the key is deliberate: a username-only key would let anyone who knows an account name lock it out from anywhere (CloudTAK's OAuth client and password-grant account in particular). The cost, accepted: a guessing run spread across many addresses gets one allowance per address.

**Read first:** `M10-00-wave-rules.md`; status notes M10-11 (the lockout list, clear action, counters and the limiter shared through `AppContext`), M10-17 (the scan finding) and M9-14 (the lockout that outlived a process; the throttled warn); R-01's notes on rate limiting (`.claude/plan/reviews/`); `rustak-server/src/auth/ratelimit.rs` and `ratelimit/{class,view}.rs`, every caller of `check`/`record_failure`/`record_success`; `rustak-server/src/config/auth.rs` (`RateLimitConfig`), `config/server.rs` (the `X-Forwarded-*` trust setting); `rustak-server/src/web/api/lockouts.rs`, `rustak-api/src/lockout.rs`, `rustak-ui/src/pages/settings_lockouts.rs`, `e2e/tests/settings.spec.ts`; `docs/deployment.md` "Sign-in lockouts". For the facts: Cormode & Muthukrishnan (count-min), Estan & Varghese (conservative update), Feng et al. (Stochastic Fair Blue), Kirsch & Mitzenmacher (double hashing).

**Deliver.**
1. **The sketch.** A fixed array of atomic cells per tier — `DEPTH` rows of `WIDTH` cells, constants with their sizing argument in the rustdoc (start from 4 × 2¹⁶; the simulation in item 8 is what justifies the choice) — allocated once, zeroed. A cell carries what a decision needs (a count, the window it belongs to, a locked-until moment), packed so that an update is a compare-and-swap; time is injected, as the current `*_at` methods do. Indices come from one keyed hash per operation (SipHash with a key drawn at process start, never logged, so nobody outside can aim at a cell) by double hashing, domain-separated per tier and prefix level. No allocation, no lock and no loop over the table on any request path.
2. **It never undercounts — under concurrency too.** N failures for one key, however they interleave across threads, count as at least N. Conservative update is wanted for its accuracy but only if that property holds; if it cannot be made to hold lock-free, use plain count-min increments and say so. Prove it with a test that hammers one key from many threads and asserts the count, and reason about it in the rustdoc.
3. **Decisions.** `check` refuses when the address is locked at tier 1 (at /32-or-/64, or at /48) or the pair is locked at tier 2 — locked meaning the minimum locked-until across the key's cells is in the future. `record_failure` counts once at tier 1 (each prefix level) and once at tier 2, and a key whose estimate reaches its threshold inside the window has its cells locked for the lockout. A window that has run out starts again, per cell. Subjects are case-folded the way account names compare; an unknown account is counted exactly like a real one. IPv4-mapped IPv6 addresses are IPv4. A missing address is a key of its own.
4. **`record_success` no longer forgets a key** — a sketch cannot delete one key without undercounting its neighbours. Keep the call sites, document the change of rule (ten failures in a window, whether or not a success came between), and find every test and behaviour that relied on a success clearing the count.
5. **Configuration.** The existing `[auth.rate_limit]` `attempts` / `window` / `lockout` keep their meaning and defaults and govern **tier 2** (the pair), so nothing an operator has set changes meaning. New keys for tier 1 with defaults of 30 failures a window per address and 300 per IPv6 /48, the same window and lockout; documented in `config.example.toml` and `docs/deployment.md`, with what each tier is for and why the address is in the pair. `deny_unknown_fields` stays.
6. **What an administrator sees (M10-11 must keep working).** A sketch cannot list its keys, so lockouts are recorded as *events* in a small bounded ring (newest first, fixed capacity, display only — never consulted for a decision): class, the key as shown, the address or prefix, when it began, when it ends, the estimate that earned it. `GET /api/v1/auth/lockouts` lists the events still in force; the per-class refusal and lockout counters stay, and a tier-1 lockout is listed as the address (or prefix) it is. `POST …/clear` zeroes that key's cells and marks the event cleared, audited as today; say in the response and the docs that clearing also forgives any key that shares those cells. Adapt `rustak-api`, the Settings → Security card and its e2e spec with the smallest change that tells the truth; the page gains the two facts an operator now needs — how full each tier is (the fraction of cells locked, sampled cheaply, not scanned per request) and that lockouts are estimates.
7. **The address the limiter sees.** Callers pass `request.peer_addr()`. Find out whether the server's `X-Forwarded-*` trust setting is honoured anywhere on the way to the limiter. Behind a reverse proxy an unresolved peer address would put every user in one tier-1 bucket, where one attacker locks out everyone. If a resolved-client-address helper already exists and is simply not used here, use it; if honouring forwarded headers would be new behaviour, do **not** add it in this brief — report exactly what you found and what a deployment behind a proxy should set meanwhile.
8. **Tests** (injected clock, no sleeping, no wall-clock bounds):
   - the properties above: never undercounts (single-threaded and concurrent); a key is refused only when all of its cells are locked; window and lockout expiry; /64 and /48 grouping; IPv4-mapped addresses; case folding; an unknown account counted like a real one;
   - **false lockouts are rare, and how rare is asserted**: a deterministic simulation (fixed hash key) that locks K distinct keys and measures the fraction of fresh keys refused, at light load (hundreds of keys: expect none) and under a flood (K near and above `WIDTH`), against the analytic `(1 − e^(−K/WIDTH))^DEPTH`; put the table in the status note and the docs;
   - **one address cannot do it alone**: with tier 1 in force, a single address trying many usernames is locked at tier 1 after its allowance and locks only a bounded number of tier-2 keys;
   - memory is fixed: the limiter's size after a million distinct failing keys is its size when new;
   - the lockout ring is bounded and newest-first; clear zeroes and audits;
   - the contract tests for the two lockout endpoints, updated.
9. **Every harness and suite that fails on purpose from one address.** Tier 1 counts *all* failures from an address, and every test suite's traffic comes from loopback: 30 a minute will be reached by suites that never tripped the pair limit. Audit `TestServer` and the integration harnesses (`testing/context.rs`, `stream_support`, `workload_support` — M10-07 raised `attempts` to 10,000 on shared servers for the same reason), `e2e/scripts/start-server.mjs`, and the configurations the interop suites start a server with (`interop/node-tak`, `interop/cloudtak`, `interop/eud`); set the tier-1 allowance explicitly where a suite needs it, and say which suites you could run and which only CI or the nightly can prove.
10. **Docs.** `docs/deployment.md` "Sign-in lockouts": the two tiers, the estimate and its error, the fail-closed behaviour under a large distributed flood and the figures from the simulation, what clear does, the proxy caveat. Module docs replace "Why the sweep is on a clock and the map has a ceiling" with why there is no map.
