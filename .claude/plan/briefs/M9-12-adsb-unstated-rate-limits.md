# M9-12 — adsb.lol refuses without naming a delay: adapt anyway, and say what actually happened

**Why — production finding, 2026-09-22 (Dublin, plugins at `7a8b6a4`).** M9-08's adaptive cadence never engaged: six `429`s in thirty polls at `poll = "10s"`, and no "asks for Ns between requests" line. The log said `The ADS-B source asked us to wait before the next request. source=adsb.lol seconds=20` — and on the earlier build, at `poll = "5s"`, the same line said `seconds=10`. The number is always **twice the configured interval**, which is `wait_for_at`'s own fallback (`asked.unwrap_or(self.interval * 2)`, `rustak-plugin-adsb/src/sources/state.rs`), so adsb.lol is answering `429` **without a usable `Retry-After`** (absent, or in a form `retry_after()` does not parse). M9-08 deliberately adapts only to a *stated* delay, so against this provider it never adapts, and the log line attributes our own guess to the server. adsb.lol documents its limits as "dynamic based on the environment load", so a stated delay may never come.

**Read first:** `.claude/plan/conventions.md`; status M9-08 (the adaptation rule and why unstated delays were excluded: compounding toward the ceiling over a week of flaky minutes) and M9-02; `rustak-plugin-adsb/src/sources/{state,notice,aggregator,mod}.rs` (`SourceState`, `wait_for_at`, `adapt`, `LIMIT_WINDOW`, `retry_after()`), `health.rs` (metrics), `README.md`, `config.example.toml`.

**Deliver (ADS-B plugin only).**
1. **Verify the hypothesis first** by reading `retry_after()`: which header forms does it accept? Make it accept integer seconds, decimal seconds (round up) and an HTTP-date (RFC 9110 §10.2.3), case-insensitively, and unit-test each. Record in your status note what form, if any, adsb.lol could be sending (you may make at most THREE single requests to `https://api.adsb.lol/v2/point/53.35/-6.26/25` with a descriptive User-Agent to look at normal response headers; do not try to provoke a 429).
2. **Adapt to unstated refusals, with recovery (AIMD).** A `429` that names no delay, arriving within `LIMIT_WINDOW` polls of the previous `429`, raises the effective interval multiplicatively (×1.5, rounded up to a whole second, capped at 120 s). After a sustained clean run — 60 consecutive successful polls — the interval steps back down one notch (÷1.5) toward, and never below, the configured or provider-default interval; another refusal resets the clean-run counter. A *stated* delay keeps M9-08's rule (two inside the window ⇒ `max(current, stated)`), and a stated delay is never decayed below while the process lives. This answers M9-08's compounding concern: a flaky minute raises the interval briefly and it recovers; a provider that keeps refusing settles at the rate it tolerates.
3. **An explicit `poll` is a floor, not a pin.** Operator-configured `poll` sets the starting and minimum interval; server behaviour may raise the effective interval above it and recovery never goes below it. Say so in `config.example.toml`, the README and the start-up line (`poll_s=10 (may be raised if the provider rate-limits)` or equivalent, once).
4. **Say what happened.** When the server named a delay: "…asked us to wait Ns". When it did not: "…refused a request (429) without naming a delay; waiting Ns" — never attribute our guess to the provider. Interval changes are logged once per change at `info`, in the `notice.rs` state-change style ("polling adsb.lol every 15s after repeated rate limits", "back to 10s after N clean polls"); no per-429 lines beyond the existing first/reminder/recovered cadence.
5. **Metrics.** `source.poll_effective_s` reflects the adapted interval; add `source.poll_configured_s`; `source.rate_limited` unchanged. The heartbeat is `healthy` while adapting (rate limiting that the plugin absorbs is not degradation) unless more than half of the last 20 polls were refused, which is `degraded` with a message.
6. **Tests** (clock-injected, no sleeps): unstated 429s every fifth poll converge upward and stop being refused in a simulated provider that tolerates one request per 18 s; the interval recovers after 60 clean polls and never drops below the configured value; a stated delay still follows M9-08's rule and is not decayed; the log-facing `Report`/message selection distinguishes stated from unstated; `retry_after()` forms; metrics fields.
